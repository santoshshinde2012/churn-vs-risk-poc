##############################################################################
PART A. CHURN
##############################################################################
randomised test months (50/50): [90, 110, 130, 150]
targeting month (standing 15% holdout): day 170
book on day 170: 16,874 accounts   counterfactual churn rate 4.99%

eligible (score >= 0.10) on day 170: 1,682
  contacted (treated): 1,439
  held out (control):  243   <- nobody touches these
pooled across all 5 months: 4,437 treated, 3,192 control

on day 170 the campaign saved 96 accounts and talked 27 into leaving.
both numbers are invisible in production. what the warehouse records is
simply whether each account churned, with no note about why.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A1. INCREMENTAL RETENTION: THE ONLY HONEST MEASURE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
churn among the 4,437 called:      38.56%   95% CI [37.13%, 39.99%]
churn among the 3,192 held out:    45.27%   95% CI [43.54%, 47.00%]

incremental retention: +6.71% +/- 2.24% (significant at 95%)

this number does not exist without the holdout. the campaign's own
numbers would say the called accounts churned less than the book average,
which is true and means nothing: they were picked for being different.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A2. THE MODEL LOOKS WRONG EXACTLY WHERE IT WAS RIGHT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
the 96 saved accounts scored 0.55 on average, well into the call band.
in the next training set they are labelled 'did not churn'. the model
gets punished for the prediction that caused the save.

next month's model, trained on the labels the campaign left behind: PR-AUC 0.717
next month's model, trained on labels no campaign touched:          PR-AUC 0.729
difference: -0.012

average score given to persuadable accounts:
  by the contaminated model: 0.4763
  by the clean model:        0.5626
  the contaminated model rates them 15.3% lower

saveable churners in tomorrow's top 200, by which labels trained the model:
  contaminated: 99
  clean:        107

this is one round. the effect is small and it is directional, which is the
dangerous combination: nothing in a monitoring dashboard fires, and the
model gets a little worse at finding the segment the programme is best at
keeping, every month, forever. the holdout is what arrests it. those rows
never got called, so their labels still say what would have happened, and
a model trained with them in it keeps a memory of the world before the
campaign existed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A3. WHO CAN BE TALKED OUT OF IT (UPLIFT, NOT RISK)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
uplift models fitted on months [90, 110, 130, 150] (2,998 contacted, 2,949 control)
applied to the 1,682 eligible accounts on day 170

composition of the top decile by churn score:
  persuadable   41.1%
  sure_thing    13.1%
  lost_cause    39.9%
  sleeping_dog  6.0%

40% of the accounts a risk model is most confident about cannot be saved:
by the time the signal is unambiguous, the decision is old. another
6% were staying until someone reminded them they were paying
for this. neither belongs on a call list, and no churn score can tell
you which is which, because both look exactly like leaving.

same budget of 200 calls, six ways of spending it
(customers kept is the simulator's truth, not an estimate):
                             strategy  customers_kept  per_100_calls  lost_causes  sleeping_dogs
                   highest risk score           19.24           9.62           73             13
           highest uplift (T-learner)           14.48           7.24           49             25
 highest uplift (transformed outcome)           16.37           8.19           52             17
highest risk, screened by uplift sign           21.88          10.94           66             12
                            at random           14.40           7.20           30             24
                  clairvoyant ceiling           72.70          36.35            0              0

screening out the accounts with a non-positive estimated uplift and then
calling the riskiest of what is left keeps +2.6 customers (+14%)
for the same 200 calls. it drops 633 of 1,682 accounts before ranking.

ranking by the same uplift estimate does worse than the plain risk score (16.4 vs 19.2).
that is not a contradiction, and it is the practical lesson in this section.
  correlation of the risk score with the true effect: +0.263
  correlation of the uplift estimate with it:         +0.011

the sign of a treatment effect is estimable from a quarter of experiments.
its magnitude, per account, is not: it is a difference between two noisy
quantities, and the noise is larger than the thing being measured. so use
the uplift model for what it can support - excluding the people a call
would waste or backfire on - and let the churn score do the ranking inside
what survives. treating an uplift score as a league table is how uplift
projects get cancelled.

and the headroom is real: a clairvoyant list keeps 73, against 22 for the best
rule here. most of that gap buys more experiments, not a better model.
  plot saved: outputs/plots/churn_uplift.png

##############################################################################
PART B. RISK
##############################################################################
the model was live over days 150-200: 62,629 transactions
  blocked: 246   of which really fraud: 242
  allowed: 62,383

blocking is also a decision not to find out. those 246 transactions never
completed, so no chargeback can ever arrive against them, and no label
will ever exist. the fraud among them is not mislabelled; it is absent.

fraud visible to the next retrain: 114 of 356 (32%)
the model is blindest on exactly the transactions it is most sure about.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B1. FOUR WAYS TO BUILD THE NEXT TRAINING SET
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
each retrained on the deployment window, then scored on days 200-240:

  naive: retrain on what was allowed         PR-AUC 0.656
  + exploration slice (2%, IPW)              PR-AUC 0.825   recovers 68% of the gap
  + review-queue labels                      PR-AUC 0.806   recovers 61% of the gap
  oracle: every outcome observed             PR-AUC 0.904

note the third row. adding review-queue labels made things worse, not
better (0.806 against 0.825), and that is the reviewers, not the queue.
they are wrong 12% of the time here, and a wrong label in the band
the model is least sure about does more damage than a missing one. use
review decisions as labels, carefully, and measure the reviewers first.

the naive retrain is the one that happens by default, because nothing
about it looks wrong: the query runs, the rows come back, the model
trains. it is 0.248 PR-AUC behind what the same window could
have produced, and it costs 2% of blocked traffic to buy most of that
back. that is the trade: a small, deliberate, budgeted amount of fraud
let through, in exchange for a model that can still see next year.

one thing an exploration slice is not: a shadow challenger. a challenger
in shadow mode scores everything and decides nothing, which makes it a
good way to compare two models on the same traffic, and no help at all
here. it inherits the champion's censored labels, because the champion
is still the one deciding what completes.

the last row is the identical statistic against a fixed reference - the

the rolling reference is the safer default anyway, because it does not
assume the flag rate is stationary, and on this stream it plainly is not:
it ranges from 0.142% to 1.101% across the monitoring window, with fraud
rings arriving in bursts. a fixed reference bakes one arbitrary fortnight
in as the definition of normal.

as configured, the alarm fires on 0 of 15 quiet days and on 11 of the 40 after.

first alert from population PSI:      never fired
first alert from the 7-day flag rate: day 203

the population PSI never fires, and that is not a bug in the monitor.
the card-testing wave is 0.72% of the transactions in that window. a
statistic computed over the whole population cannot see a change that
small, and lowering the threshold until it does would produce an alert
every week for reasons that are never attacks.

the flag rate sees it because the attack is concentrated exactly where
the flag rate looks: the top of the score distribution.
